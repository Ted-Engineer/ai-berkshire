VST Q2 FY2026 earnings call

Summary

Vistra Corp delivered second-quarter 2026 Adjusted EBITDA of $1.767 billion, a more than 30% increase from $1.35 billion in the year-ago quarter, reflecting strong execution across both generation and retail. The company reaffirmed its full-year 2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance of $6.8–$7.6 billion and Adjusted Free Cash Flow before Growth of $3.925–$4.725 billion, with management expressing confidence in achieving results at or above the midpoint.

The year-over-year improvement was driven by a 68% jump in generation EBITDA to $994 million, benefiting from favorable hedging, higher PJM capacity revenues, optimized flexible gas generation, the restart of Martin Lake Unit 1, and contributions from the Lotus acquisition. Retail EBITDA rose modestly to $773 million, with seasonal strength underscoring the integrated model's resilience. Average realized power prices were approximately 5% higher per MWh versus last year.

Operationally, Vistra completed its spring maintenance cycle, including three nuclear refueling outages and 92 gas/coal outages, achieving over 97% commercial availability during record summer heat. PJM and ERCOT set new all-time peak loads (168 GW and 91 GW, respectively) in July, reinforcing the structurally improved demand environment. Data center development remains active, highlighted by the new Helix Digital Infrastructure partnership with KKR, NVIDIA, and KIA, where Vistra will commit up to $1 billion and serve as preferred power partner.

Management maintained the 2027 Adjusted EBITDA midpoint opportunity of $7.4–$7.8 billion despite softer ERCOT forwards, citing PJM strength, hedging, and nuclear PTC downside protection. The company plans to allocate $4.5–$5 billion to growth investments, including Cogentrix, Permian Peakers, and nuclear PPAs, while continuing shareholder returns. With over $10 billion of available cash in 2026–2027, Vistra retains $2–$2.5 billion for additional discretionary allocation.

Key risks include near-term ERCOT price softness from battery additions and regulatory uncertainty surrounding data center audits and PJM market design. However, Vistra is well positioned to benefit from multi-year load growth of 4–6% in ERCOT and 2–3% in PJM, with an improving investment-grade credit profile and disciplined capital allocation.

  • Retail delivered strong seasonal performance in the second quarter.

Regional Trends

  • PJM and ERCOT recorded record peak loads in July: PJM >168 GW, ERCOT >91 GW.
  • ERCOT forward prices have softened; PJM forwards strengthened, partially offsetting.

Operational Metrics

  • Fleet commercial availability >97% during heat waves.
  • Completed 3 nuclear refueling outages and 92 gas/coal planned outages.
  • Since 2021, retired 171M shares at average $38/share; returned $6.5B via buybacks.

Outlook

Official guidance:

  • 2026 Adjusted EBITDA: $6.8–$7.6 billion
  • 2026 Adjusted FCF before Growth: $3.925–$4.725 billion
  • Management expects to deliver at or above the midpoint for 2026.
  • 2027 Adjusted EBITDA midpoint opportunity: $7.4–$7.8 billion, excluding Cogentrix and Meta PPAs, which could add ~$700 million.

Management commentary:

  • Helix partnership: up to $1 billion commitment, with amounts above $500 million subject to milestones; Vistra is preferred power partner.
  • Growth investments of $4.5–$5 billion include Cogentrix, Permian Peakers, PJM nuclear (Meta PPAs), Oak Hill 2 solar, and Helix.
  • Approximately $2–$2.5 billion of additional cash available through 2027; share repurchase authorization of $1.2 billion expected to be exhausted by end-2027, with flexibility to increase.
  • Long-term goal: mid-investment-grade credit ratings from all three agencies (currently two).

Q&A Highlights

Q (Constantine, Wells Fargo): Impact of Texas data center audits, potential delays, and Batch Zero on near-term outlook and hedging for 2027-2028? A: Long-term ERCOT load forecast unchanged (115-120 GW by 2030). Audit may pause reviews for a few months, but Comanche Peak (energize end-2027) not affected; support queue thinning. Comprehensive hedging mitigates near-term softness.

Q (Jeremy, J.P. Morgan): Relative dynamics between PJM and ERCOT and where more contracts are being secured? A: PJM is tighter; ERCOT less tight at present. Both markets show high customer interest; regulatory uncertainty differs. Customers don't need perfect clarity to execute contracts.

Q (Jeremy, J.P. Morgan): PJM strategy regarding RBP, bilateral contracts, and preferences? A: Bilateral discussions are viable; willing buyer-seller. RBP cap of 555 may not support all new builds; bilaterals could reduce reliance on capacity market. Cost escalation is a challenge.

Q (Jeremy, J.P. Morgan): Thoughts on IRAS proposal? A: Prefer incentive-based flexibility (carrot) over mandates (stick). Concerns about curtailment before paid resources. Will weigh in when filed.

Q (Michael, Wolfe Research): Helix platform mechanics, milestones, and how it interacts with existing commercial discussions? A: Additive channel to evaluate more deals; leverages Vistra assets and KKR capital. Milestones trigger additional $500M investment. No requirement to use Helix for every project.

Q (Michael, Wolfe Research): Drivers for 2026 midpoint-or-better and bias for 2027 range? A: Strong first half positions 2026 at/above midpoint. 2027 trending toward lower end due to ERCOT headwinds, offset by PJM, hedging, PTC. Excluded transactions could add ~$700M.

Q (James, Melius Research): Helix capital raising, additional partners, potential IPO? A: Expect more investors over time. Each deal flexible; Vistra can bring Helix as equity partner or self-fund. No IPO discussion currently.

Q (James, Melius Research): Timeline for Abbott's audit/queue process? A: No formal moratorium; pause on Batch Zero updates. Expect audits complete in a couple months. Baseload projects like Comanche Peak not delayed; near-term energizations could be affected.

Q (Carly, Goldman Sachs): Capital allocation flexibility and willingness to expand buyback authorization? A: $1.2B authorization remains; expect to exhaust by end-2027. Management/Board open to increasing if market conditions warrant; may seek additional authorization to maintain $1B for 2027.

Q (Carly, Goldman Sachs): Hedge update for 2028 across regions? A: No detailed hedging commentary. Diversification helps: PJM strength offsets ERCOT weakness. ERCOT softness is recency bias; battery returns disappointed, slowing future additions.

Q (David, Morgan Stanley): Helix pipeline and return targets vs internal? A: Early days, but close collaboration with KKR. Pipeline includes existing and new opportunities. Projects must meet mid-teens levered returns. Helix may accept different risk appetite, broadening optionality.

Q (David, Morgan Stanley): Other Batch Zero projects beyond Comanche Peak? A: Yes, multiple projects in Batch Zero baseload, to-be-studied, and Batch One. No specific details disclosed. Baseload projects expected to move forward; to-be-studied faces more uncertainty.

Q (Rini, Bank of America): Timeline and uncertainty for IRAS/co-location proceeding? A: FERC's June co-location order was positive; PJM/TOs requested more time for compliance filing (likely 30-60 days). Order adopted many Vistra positions. Expect clarity on rates and services soon.

Q (Rini, Bank of America): Appetite for energy-only vs energy+capacity contracts in PJM? A: Customers still prefer both energy and capacity to power data centers. Rising prices drive interest in locking in costs. No significant shift to energy-only deals.
